# Who checks the score your customer is asked to trust?

AI systems are trained, ranked, and sold on scores produced by verifiers, judges, reward models, and label sets. When people optimize against a score, its blind spots can become targets.

## What an independent assessment does

A review begins by freezing the exact version of the evaluation and evidence under review. We define the claim before looking at findings, test a pre-specified set of failure modes, and use an independent reference path.

Where human judgment is relevant, that means fresh blind review with disagreement and uncertainty reported rather than hidden. The report shows what we found, how we found it, and what the review does not cover.

### The review record

1. **Freeze the evidence.** Record the task, dataset, and split version; relevant source or synthetic-data lineage; verifier or judge configuration; rubric, prompt, and model version; selection rule; relevant traces; and review date.
2. **Specify the test.** State the claim, failure modes, sample, and rule for a finding before examining the result.
3. **Use a separate reference path.** Inspect the system, replay evidence, or conduct blind review as the claim requires.
4. **Publish the limits.** State what the review covered, what it could not inspect, and what a result does not establish.

## Who this is for

The initial research focus is teams that sell RL environments, training data, or evaluation systems to another organization. This includes preference-data, reward, and synthetic-data pipelines when a customer must rely on the labels, selection policy, or performance claim they produce.

The question is most acute when a customer, procurement team, or other outside party must rely on a score produced by a rubric, an LLM judge, or expert labels rather than a fully checkable rule.

## A necessary limit

If a claim is fully and objectively checkable—for example, a date was extracted correctly or a test passed—you may not need us. The service is most useful where a buyer has to rely on a score standing in for a human or semantic judgment they did not design or observe.
